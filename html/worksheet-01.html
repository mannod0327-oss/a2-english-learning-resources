Lesson

Present simple

  • Use it for habits, routines, feelings and facts.
  • I / you / we / they talk · he / she / it talks
  • Negative: I don't talk · She doesn't talk · Question: Do you talk? Does he talk?
  • Spelling: go → goes, do → does, watch → watches, study → studies
  • Time words: always, usually, often, sometimes, never (before the main verb, after be) · every day, on Mondays, at weekends
  • Doimiy, takrorlanadigan harakatlar va umumiy faktlar. He/she/it bilan fe'lga -s qo'shiladi.

There is / There are

  • Use it to say that something exists or is in a place.
  • There is a bike in the street. · There are two men in the photo.
  • Negative: There isn't … / There aren't … · Question: Is there …? Are there …?
  • After the first mention, use it or they: There's a bike outside. It's my sister's.
  • Biror joyda nimadir borligini aytish uchun: there is (birlik), there are (ko'plik).

It's

  • Use it as the subject for weather, time, days, distance and situations:
  • It's cold today. · It's nine o'clock. · It's Friday! · Is it far to your school? · It's great to see you.
  • Ob-havo, vaqt, sana va masofa haqida gapirganda gap it bilan boshlanadi.

Reading: Life in Chimyon

Dilshod lives in Chimyon, a small village in the mountains near Tashkent. There is a school, a small shop and a mosque in the centre of the village, but there aren't any cinemas.

Dilshod gets up at six o'clock every day. He helps his father with the sheep and then he walks to school. It's about two kilometres from his house. In winter it's very cold and there is a lot of snow, so the walk takes a long time. His sister, Nilufar, doesn't walk. She goes to school by bike.

At weekends many tourists come to the village because they love skiing. Dilshod often works in his uncle's café on Saturdays. 'It's hard work, but I like it,' he says.

  5. What does Dilshod often do on Saturdays?
